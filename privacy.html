Privacy Policy

What these applications collect, store, and send — and what they don't

This policy covers the desktop applications built and distributed from this site and its linked repositories, including Zarya. These are personal, independently-developed tools, not a company or a service with a backend — there is no server collecting anything, because there is no server.

The general rule

  • No analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting, no tracking of any kind. Nothing about how you use these apps is recorded anywhere, by anyone.
  • No account with me is required or possible. I do not receive, see, or store your data — everything lives on your own machine.
  • Data only moves between your machine and the third-party service an app is explicitly built to talk to (e.g. Google Calendar, Open-Meteo for weather), directly, using credentials only you hold.

Zarya specifically

Zarya is a personal daily-dashboard app (system updates, weather, backup status, calendar). Here is exactly what each part does:

  • Google Calendar: if you connect it, Zarya requests read-only access (calendar.readonly) to fetch today's events, directly from Google's API, using an OAuth token that lives only on your machine, in your system's secure keyring (KWallet/GNOME Keyring via libsecret). I never see this token, your calendar data, or anything else about your Google account — there is no server in the middle to see it. Disconnecting in Zarya's Preferences removes the token from your keyring; you can also revoke access entirely at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Weather: the city you enter is sent to Open-Meteo (a free, keyless weather API) to fetch a forecast. No account, identifier, or tracking cookie is involved — it's an anonymous HTTP request, same as any browser loading a page.
  • System updates and backup status: these run entirely on your own machine (zypper, flatpak, systemctl) and never leave it.
  • Everything Zarya stores — your city, unit preference, last-update result, OAuth token — stays in your local config, cache, and keyring directories. None of it is sent to me or to any third party I control.

If something here is wrong

If any of this is unclear, or you find a place where an app does something other than what's described here, write to calstfrancis@disroot.org and I will fix either the app or this page.
